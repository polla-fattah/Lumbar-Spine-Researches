\section{Detailed Quantitative Error Analysis \& Taxonomy}
Analysis of false positive and false negative extractions revealed three primary categories of linguistic ambiguity in the local narrative report corpus:

\subsection{Multi-Level List Distributivity \& Range Ambiguity}
In reports containing sentences such as \textit{"L4-5 and L5-S1 discs show circumferential bulge"}, clause-bound level binding correctly extracted bulges for both levels ($L4$--$L5$ and $L5$--$S1$). However, false negatives ($FN = 111$ for disc bulge) occurred primarily when radiologists wrote non-standard level ranges (e.g., \textit{"L2 down to L5 discs show generalized bulge"}), which sentence-token matching failed to expand.

\subsection{Negation Scope Boundaries}
Clause-bound negation splitting on conjunction delimiters (\textit{"but"}, \textit{"however"}, \textit{";"}) prevented negation triggers from leaking across clause boundaries. False positives ($FP = 73$ for canal stenosis) were driven primarily by anatomical noun conflation where general \textit{"neural foraminal stenosis"} was incorrectly matched as central canal stenosis.

\subsection{Lexical Variance \& Synonym Gaps}
The zero recall ($0.000$) for Facet Joint Arthrosis highlights a critical synonym gap: local narrative reports frequently described facet joint degeneration using terms such as \textit{"facet hypertrophy"} or \textit{"posterior element degenerative changes"}, which were unmapped in the initial keyword regex rule set.

\section{Extracted Cohort Findings Summary}
The extracted dataset confirmed peak degeneration burden concentrated at \textbf{L4--L5 (61.5\% disc bulge, 26.7\% canal stenosis)}, followed by L3--L4 (33.8\%) and L5--S1 (28.2\%).
